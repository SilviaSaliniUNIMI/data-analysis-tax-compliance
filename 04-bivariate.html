Counts and conditional percentages answer different questions

600 fictional filings are classified by communication channel and filing outcome at a fixed observation date. These categories are mutually exclusive. Channel allocation is observational.

Worked answer

The cell count is 150. The row share is 150/260 = 57.69%; the column share is also 150/260 = 57.69% because these particular margins happen to agree; the overall share is 150/600 = 25%. Equal numerical values need not have the same meaning. Compare Email × Late to see different row and column percentages.

SECTION 03 / 11

Compare observed counts with independence

Eᵢⱼ = (row total × column total)/n
χ² = Σ (Oᵢⱼ−Eᵢⱼ)²/Eᵢⱼ;   df = (r−1)(c−1)

H₀: channel and outcome are independent in the target population. H₁: they are associated. The 3 × 3 table gives 4 degrees of freedom.

Worked cell and assumptions

E = 260×260/600 = 112.6667; O−E = 37.3333; contribution = 12.3708. All nine contributions sum to χ² = 68.1374. Under H₀ and the conditions, the reference distribution is approximately χ² with 4 df. Observations must be independent, categories well defined and expected counts sufficiently large. All expected counts here exceed 5. Sparse 2 × 2 tables may call for Fisher’s exact test.

Residual signs locate over- and under-representation; they are descriptive diagnostics, not automatically multiplicity-adjusted cell tests.

SECTION 04 / 11

Association strength is different from evidence

V = √[χ² / {n min(r−1,c−1)}]

V is between 0 and 1. The expression without the square root is V². It has no sign and does not show which cells differ.

Imagine a larger independent sample with exactly the same proportions. Multiplying counts here illustrates that comparison. Duplicating the same records would not create independent evidence and would invalidate the test.

Report conditional percentages and an effect size alongside a p-value. Generalisation also depends on how the filings entered the dataset. The classroom sample is constructed; its p-value illustrates the calculation under an independent sampling model.

SECTION 05 / 11

A quantitative outcome across procedures

Three fictional processing procedures each have six observations. Begin with distributions, not just a test. The values were constructed for arithmetic transparency.

Download the 18 processing times

The default groups have means 5, 9 and 13 days; each has sample variance 2 days². Controls translate the group means and scale within-group deviations.

Two groups: independent or paired?

For two independent groups, compare means using a two-sample t test. Welch’s version estimates variances separately. The pooled equal-variance t test is equivalent to classical one-way ANOVA with two groups: F = t² and identical two-sided p-values. For before/after measurements on the same cases, analyse within-case differences with a paired test; do not treat the pairs as independent groups.

SECTION 06 / 11

Separate between-group and within-group variation

SST = SSB + SSW
SSB = Σ nⱼ(x̄ⱼ−x̄)²;   SSW = ΣΣ(xᵢⱼ−x̄ⱼ)²
F = [SSB/(k−1)] / [SSW/(n−k)]

H₀: μA = μB = μC. H₁: at least one population mean differs. The table follows the settings in the previous section.

η² = SSB / SST
Conditions and follow-up

Classical ANOVA assumes independent observations, normal errors within groups and equal population error variances. Check group distributions and residuals; independence comes from the design. With unequal variances, consider Welch ANOVA. A significant F test does not identify the differing pairs. Use justified contrasts or multiplicity-adjusted comparisons such as Tukey under the classical model.

With two factors, an interaction means the association with one factor changes across levels of the other. That is an extension beyond this one-way activity.

SECTION 07 / 11

Look at the scatterplot before summarising it

Use document count and review time for a fictional sample of cases, then examine deliberately constructed counterexamples. All displays below are descriptive.

sXY = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ)/(n−1)
r = sXY/(sXsY)

Covariance uses the product of the variables’ units. Pearson r is dimensionless and measures linear association. It is undefined if either variable is constant. It is not a slope and is sensitive to influential points.

Contribution and population distinction

A case contributes (xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ): matching signs give a positive product, opposite signs a negative one. Dividing covariance by both SDs cancels the common n−1 factors. Population correlation ρ is a parameter; sample correlation r estimates it. For i.i.d. bivariate normal data, H₀: ρ = 0 can be tested using t = r√[(n−2)/(1−r²)] with n−2 df. These normal-model conditions concern inference, not the descriptive calculation of r.

SECTION 09 / 11

Optional: from association to a fitted line

This section always uses the base workload sample, independently of the scatterplot scenario selected earlier. Return to this optional section before supervised learning.

ŷ = b₀ + b₁x;   b₁ = sXY/sX²;   b₀ = ȳ−b₁x̄
SSE = Σ(yᵢ−ŷᵢ)²;   R² = 1−SSE/SST = r²

OLS minimises squared vertical residuals. R² = r² applies to simple OLS with an intercept. A coefficient describes an association in the units of the variables; it is not automatically causal.

The residual SD (SER) is √[SSE/(n−2)], in minutes. The prediction interval for one new case adds individual variation and is wider than the confidence interval for the mean response.

Assumptions, diagnostics and formulas

For the classical intervals used here: E(Y|X) is linear, errors are independent with constant variance, and normal errors give exact small-sample t inference. No marginal normality assumption on X is needed. Plot residuals to look for curvature and changing spread; investigate influential observations.

h₀ = 1/n + (x₀−x̄)² / Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²
Mean CI: ŷ₀ ± t.975,n−2 SER √h₀
New case PI: ŷ₀ ± t.975,n−2 SER √(1+h₀)

For a slope test, t = b₁/SE(b₁), with SE(b₁) = SER/√Σ(xᵢ−x̄)². The simple-regression global F equals this t². Heteroskedasticity requires suitable methods, such as robust coefficient standard errors; merely reporting R² does not validate the model.

Predictive performance needs evaluation on new or held-out cases. A high training R² and a small slope p-value do not establish good out-of-sample prediction.
