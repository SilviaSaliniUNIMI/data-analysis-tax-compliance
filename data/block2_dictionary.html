Block 2: synthetic datasets

These are separate teaching cases, not tables to merge by row number. They contain no real taxpayer records and do not describe national tax rates, legal deadlines or observed policy effects. The original 24-firm descriptive dataset is separate again.

1. Finite register: sampling experiment

tax_register_1000.csv: 1,000 fictional filings. record_id is an identifier; declared_kEUR is a positive declared amount in thousand EUR; priority = 1 selects the largest-amount quarter (250 records); discrepancy = 1 marks a simulated discrepancy. All fields are complete. The generator uses a lognormal amount model and a higher discrepancy probability within the priority group. The realised full-register proportion is 0.19; the priority-group proportion is 0.38. These are features of the constructed dataset.

The interactive activity samples without replacement within each draw. Whole-register simple random sampling is compared with random selection only inside the priority group. Finite-population standard errors use the full register, N = 1,000, with population variance divisor N. A priority-only sample does not estimate the full-register target without further assumptions.

2. Processing sample: one mean

processing_sample.csv: 25 case_id values and processing_days. These manually constructed integer times are used as a fictional independent sample. The one-sample t calculation treats the data as approximately normal; exact t inference requires independent normal observations. Mean 11.4, sample SD 2.380476. The 10-day benchmark is illustrative, not a legal rule. No missing values.

3. Communication: two categorical variables

communication_counts.csv: channel (Email, SMS, Letter) and counts on_time, late, not_received. There are 600 fictional filings. Outcomes are mutually exclusive at a fixed observation date; not_received is a known outcome category. The counts are constructed, not observed. The χ² illustration assumes independent filings from a target population and does not establish causation. All expected counts exceed 5. No missing counts.

4. Processing groups: one-way ANOVA

processing_groups.csv: 18 case_id values; procedure A/B/C (categorical); processing_days (quantitative). Six observations per procedure, means 5/9/13, sample variance 2 in each. Constructed values make the decomposition transparent. The classical inference is illustrative under independent normal errors and equal population variances. Sliders create hypothetical variations of these data. No missing values.

5. Workload: correlation and regression

workload_sample.csv: 15 case_id values, documents (integer count, 1–15) and review_minutes (positive). Times follow a constructed linear pattern plus fixed deviations. No random sampling or causal identification is claimed. Classical regression intervals in the optional activity illustrate an independent, homoskedastic normal-error model; its assumptions must be assessed in real applications. The artificial influential point and mathematical counterexamples are not rows of this CSV. No missing values.

6. Reminder mix: Simpson’s paradox

reminder_mix_counts.csv: support = Reminder/Standard; complexity = Simple/Complex; on_time = count; total = stratum count. Four rows describe 200 hypothetical filings. Counts deliberately illustrate a reversal. Each support group has 100 cases, but its complexity mix differs. Sliders alter weights while holding conditional rates fixed. This is an observational comparison, not evidence of a reminder’s causal effect.

R lessons and scripts will be supplied separately. These CSV files are already available for inspection with a spreadsheet or any statistical software.
