An ordered risk band would be ordinal. Celsius temperature is an example of an interval scale: differences have meaning, but ratios depend on the chosen zero.

Compare your reasoning

Identifiers are arbitrary labels, so their numerical average has no substantive meaning. In an invoice-level dataset, firms with more invoices contribute more rows. An average across invoices and an average across firms answer different questions.

Validity: does a measure capture the intended concept? Filing delay measures timeliness, not every aspect of compliance. Reliability: would the same rule applied to the same records give consistent results? Consistent measurement can still miss the intended concept.
Frequencies, denominators and missing values
From E-tivity 1: district A pays 120 of 160 assessed units (75%); B pays 36 of 40 (90%). The combined ratio is 156/200 = 78%, not the unweighted mean 82.5%. More revenue and a higher payment ratio are different results. This is a separate illustrative scenario from the 24-firm dataset below.

Counts and proportions

For category j, pj = nj / n. Proportions sum to 1 when the categories cover the denominator.

Unknown is not zero

Two delay values are missing. Zero means a known on-time filing. Audit outcomes are only observed when audited = 1.

Worked answer

18 filings are known to be late and 22 have a known delay: 18/22 = 81.8% among observed delays. The fraction 18/24 = 75% is a lower bound for the full set, not an assumption that the missing records were on time. If both missing records were late, the full proportion would be 20/24 = 83.3%.

The same values can tell different visual stories

Illustrative annual tax receipts rise from 9.8 to 10.5 million monetary units. Before moving the slider, calculate the increase.

The change is always 0.7 million, about 7.14%. Bar length normally needs a zero baseline. A clearly labelled restricted axis may be appropriate for a line or dot plot, where full bar length is not the encoding.

VariableUseful displayKeep this information
Nominal: sectorBarsDistinct categories and a common baseline
Ordinal: satisfactionOrdered barsThe natural order from low to high
Discrete: 0, 1, 2, 5, 10 casesSticks or dotsActual numerical distances
Continuous: processing timeHistogram or boxplotDistribution shape and measurement units
Three frequent mistakes
  • One bar per distinct measurement: when most values occur once, equal-height bars hide the distribution. Use a dotplot or suitable bins.
  • Counts presented as rates: 120 payments and 36 payments say nothing about the fraction due without denominators. Recall the district ratios 75% and 90%.
  • A pie chart for multiple responses: among 100 taxpayers, 65 used online help, 45 telephone help and 30 a service desk. The 140 selections overlap. Show the three percentages as bars with denominator 100; do not normalise them into slices of a fictitious whole. A pie requires mutually exclusive parts of one total. Many similar slices are hard to compare even when valid.

SECTION 03 / 10

Intervals, density and grouped observations

Use a bar chart for categories. Use a histogram to show the distribution of a numerical variable. Bin choice changes the visible detail.

Histogram of the 24 synthetic turnover values. The last bin includes its right endpoint.

Unequal intervals

When widths differ, height must be a frequency density so that area represents count (or relative frequency).

height = count / bin width
Interpreting this distribution

Turnover has a long right tail, including values 120 and 300. A large number of bins can create empty intervals and visual noise in a small dataset. Wide bins hide local detail. In unequal-width histograms, count heights would give wide bins disproportionate area.

The modal interval uses density when widths differ
IntervalCountWidthDensity
[0,10)18101.80
[10,15)1252.40
[15,30]20151.33

The largest count is in [15,30], but the greatest concentration per unit of width is in [10,15). This is the modal interval of the density histogram.

A median estimated from grouped data

Processing times have intervals [0,12), [12,24), [24,36), [36,48] hours and counts 8, 15, 12, 5. Cumulative counts are 8, 23, 35, 40. The 50% point lies in [12,24).

Median ≈ 12 + [(20 − 8) / 15] × 12 = 21.6 hours

This linear interpolation assumes a uniform spread within the interval. The raw-data median is not known exactly, and the class midpoint 18 is not automatically the median.

SECTION 04 / 10

Mode, median and mean

The mode is the most frequent value or category. The median is the middle position after sorting. The mean is the total divided by n. Several modes can occur.

Move the largest value in this separate 12-firm example. The other 11 values are 12, 14, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 22, 24 and 26 (thousand EUR).

A weighted mean

Office A has 8 on-time filings out of 10. Office B has 45 out of 90. The average office rate is (0.80 + 0.50)/2 = 65%. The overall filing rate is (8 + 45)/100 = 53%.

weighted mean = Σ wᵢxᵢ / Σ wᵢ

Choose the weights to match the observational unit and question.

The mean redistributes the total equally and balances signed deviations: Σ(xᵢ − x̄) = 0. The median needs ordered observations. For an even number of numerical values, average the central two. For ordinal categories, identify the central category or categories without averaging arbitrary codes.

Same mode, different ordinal medians

Ratings run from Very poor, Poor, Satisfactory, Good to Excellent. A has counts (3,7,35,30,25), with cumulative counts (3,10,45,75,100). B has counts (25,30,35,7,3), with cumulative counts (25,55,90,97,100). Both have mode Satisfactory. Their median categories are Good and Poor. The mode hides this difference.

Weights, robustness and distribution shape

Two offices have 10 and 90 filings with on-time rates 80% and 50%. The combined rate is (8 + 45)/100 = 53%; 65% describes the unweighted average of the two office rates.

The median resists a small number of extreme observations. The mean remains useful when the total or a redistribution interpretation matters. Right tails often give mean > median, but similar means and medians do not prove symmetry. Inspect the graph, including possible multiple modes. If all values occur equally often, there is no unique mode.

SECTION 05 / 10

Quantiles and boxplots

We use R type-7 quantiles. With sorted observations, set h = 1 + (n−1)p and interpolate between the neighbouring positions. Other conventions can give different small-sample results.

Example: for the turnover lower quartile, h = 6.75. Values 6 and 7 are 25 and 28, so Q1 = 25 + 0.75 × (28−25) = 27.25.

Fences: Q1 − 1.5 × IQR and Q3 + 1.5 × IQR. Whiskers reach the most extreme observed values inside the fences. Points beyond are flagged for investigation.

Worked answer

Q1 = 27.25, Q3 = 61.25, IQR = 34. The upper fence is 112.25, so 120 and 300 are flagged. They may be valid large firms. Check their origin, and compare analyses before deciding what to do.

The plot here uses type-7 quartiles. Base R boxplot() can use different hinges. The supplied R script draws the same convention as this page.

A percentile describes position among observations. The 90th percentile is not 90% of the largest value. Ties and sample-quantile conventions affect exact finite-sample percentages.

SECTION 06 / 10

Building variance from deviations

Begin with four processing times: 20, 40, 0, 70 days. The mean is 32.5. Signed deviations add to zero, so they cannot measure total spread. Squaring them gives a nonnegative contribution from every observation.

Variance uses squared days; SD returns to days. Change the last value and observe how both the mean and the squared deviations change.

Range uses the endpoints. IQR describes the middle 50% by quantile positions. Absolute or squared deviations measure distances from a chosen centre.

vn = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / n
s² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n−1),   s = √s²

Use vn to describe the observed set with average squared deviations. The n−1 version is an unbiased estimator of population variance under independent, identically distributed sampling with finite variance. R var() and sd() use n−1.

Units and relative spread

If y = a + bx, mean(y) = a + b mean(x), SD(y) = |b| SD(x), and variance(y) = b² variance(x). The mean absolute deviation here is Σ|xᵢ − x̄|/n. It is not the median absolute deviation (MAD).

CV = s/x̄ measures relative spread for positive ratio-scale variables with a meaningful zero. It becomes unstable near a zero mean and is generally unsuitable for Celsius temperatures.

SECTION 07 / 10

Diversity across categories

Change three category counts. The proportions always use their current total. The number of possible categories stays K = 3, even if a count is zero.

G = 1 − Σ pj²
H = −Σ pj ln(pj)

Use 0 ln 0 = 0. G is the Gini–Simpson index (Gini impurity), not the income-inequality Gini coefficient.

For fixed K, 0 ≤ G ≤ 1−1/K and 0 ≤ H ≤ ln(K). The default uses natural logarithms (nats); use the selector to compare bases. With base 2 the unit is bits and the upper bound is log₂ K. Normalised entropy H/ln(K) lies in [0,1] for K > 1.

Compare your reasoning

A single occupied category gives G = H = 0. Equal counts maximise both: G = 2/3 and H = ln 3 ≈ 1.0986. For (12,8,4), G ≈ 0.6111 and H ≈ 1.0114. Unnormalised entropy can exceed 1. Gini–Simpson is at most 1−1/K for a finite K.

For K > 1, normalised Gini–Simpson is G / (1 − 1/K). Normalised entropy is H / log(K), using the same logarithm base above and below. With K = 3 and counts (12,8,4), these are approximately 0.9167 and 0.9206.
Classification and a connection to learning

Splitting one category changes the question. Counts (50,30,20) give G = 0.62; splitting the last category into (50,30,10,10) gives G = 0.64, even though no units were added. Compare indices only after examining category definitions and K. Normalisation does not make different classifications equivalent.

Shannon also responds to rare categories: compare (50,25,25) with (49,25,25,1). The indices need not rank every pair of distributions identically. In classification trees, Gini impurity and entropy describe class mixtures within nodes. A diverse distribution of sectors does not by itself imply good tax compliance.

SECTION 08 / 10

Relative position and composite indices

zᵢ = (xᵢ − x̄) / s,   s > 0

Using sample SD gives standardised values with sample mean 0 and sample SD 1, calculated on the same complete observations. A positive z-score means above the mean. Standardisation does not imply normality.

Constructing an index

Adding standardised variables gives them comparable units, but you still need to justify their meaning, direction and weights. Correlated inputs can count similar information more than once. Standardisation does not validate the construct.

For a constant variable, s = 0 and the usual z-score is undefined. In later prediction tasks, calculate preprocessing parameters on training data and apply those parameters to held-out data.

Optional probability bridge: Monty Hall

Three doors hide one car and two goats. The car position and initial choice are random. The host knows the car location, always opens an unchosen goat door and always offers a switch.
